# Resume Tailored to a Job Description

Format reference for step 4 of the [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) workflow. The job
description is the spec; the candidate's history is the only permitted source of
content. Step 5's evidence check is stricter here than anywhere else: never fabricate,
never inflate.

## 1. Analyse the job description

Extract into three priority tiers:

- **P1 deal-breakers** — years, must-have skills, education, certifications
- **P2 strongly desired** — methodologies, domain experience, tools
- **P3 bonus** — nice-to-haves, cultural signals

Plus: repeated keywords and phrases (ATS terms), exact job-title wording, soft-skill
language, and anything signalling company values.

## 2. Map experience to requirements

For each P1/P2 requirement, record the matching evidence from the candidate's history —
direct match, transferable equivalent, or **gap**. Gaps are handled by de-emphasis or an
explicit note in recommendations; never by invention.

## 3. Build the document

| Section | Rules |
|---|---|
| Header | name, email, phone, LinkedIn/portfolio — nothing else |
| Professional summary | 3–4 lines: years in target field, top 3–4 P1 skills, industry, unique value |
| Skills | grouped by the JD's own categories; required tools first; exact JD terminology; only substantiated skills |
| Experience | reverse-chronological; reorder bullets so the most relevant land first; **[action verb] + what + how/why + result**; quantify (%, time, scale) |
| Education | degrees, relevant certifications, coursework only if early-career |
| Optional | certifications, publications, awards, projects, volunteer work — only when they serve the JD |

Length: 1 page under 10 years' experience, 2 pages beyond; never 3.

## 4. ATS constraints

Standard section headings (`Professional Experience`, `Education`, `Skills`). Keywords
from the JD used naturally, including acronym + full form (`SQL (Structured Query
Language)`). No tables, text boxes, graphics, headers/footers, or columns. Standard
fonts, simple bullets, consistent spacing. Match the JD's job-title terminology where
truthful.

## 5. Do / don't

**Do:** be truthful; use industry-standard terminology; quantify achievements; tailor
per application; proofread for tense and consistency; keep verbs in past tense for past
roles.

**Don't:** first-person pronouns; age, marital status, or photo unless asked;
"references available upon request"; every job from a 20-year career; a generic
template with the name swapped; claims the interview could not substantiate.

## 6. Variants

| Candidate | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Career changer | hybrid/functional layout; transferable-skill summary; projects and coursework forward |
| Recent graduate | education first; coursework, internships, leadership; GPA only if ≥3.5 |
| Senior executive | executive summary; strategic impact, team scale, revenue/growth; board and speaking |
| Technical | skills section high; languages, frameworks, GitHub/portfolio; methodologies |
| Creative | portfolio link; campaign outcomes; tools; slightly bolder layout **within ATS limits** |

## 7. Deliver with recommendations

Hand over the resume plus four short analyses:

1. **Strengths** — what makes this candidate competitive for *this* JD
2. **Gaps** — P1/P2 requirements unmet, with a concrete way to address each
3. **Interview hooks** — 3–4 talking points mapped to JD requirements
4. **Cover-letter openings** — 2–3 first lines grounded in real achievements

Iterate on emphasis, tone, section set, and alternate versions for multiple
applications. Flag for the user to review before submitting, and remind them to strip
anything they do not want an employer to see.
