# Content Creation

> **Routing:** For deal-specific sales assets built from deal context, use **create-an-asset**; for interactive web tools or apps, use **web-artifact-and-app-building**; for multi-email lifecycle flows (onboarding, nurture, win-back), use **email-sequence**; for a site-wide SEO audit, use **seo-audit**; for auditing a finished draft against the brand voice and style guide, use **brand-review**.

> If you see `~~connector` placeholders, that source is optional — skip the step when it is not available.

Generate marketing content drafts tailored to a specific content type, audience, and brand voice, using the type templates, channel best practices, and formulas below.

## Workflow

### 1. Gather Inputs

Gather the following from the user. If not provided, ask before proceeding:

1. **Content type** — blog post, social media post (which platform), email newsletter, landing page copy, press release, or case study
2. **Topic** — the subject or theme of the content
3. **Target audience** — who this is for (role, industry, seniority, pain points)
4. **Key messages** — 2-4 main points or takeaways to communicate
5. **Tone** — e.g., authoritative, conversational, inspirational, technical, witty (optional if brand voice is configured)
6. **Length** — target word count or format constraint (e.g., "1000 words", "280 characters", "3 paragraphs")

### 2. Apply Brand Voice

- If the user has a brand voice configured in their local settings, apply it automatically and inform the user.
- If none is configured, ask: "Do you have brand voice guidelines you'd like me to follow? If not, I'll use a neutral professional tone."
- Apply the specified or default tone consistently throughout the draft.

### 3. Draft by Type

Use the **Type Templates** below as the structural baseline, then apply the **Channel Best Practices** for the target channel and the **Headline and Hook Formulas** / **Call-to-Action Best Practices** for the opening and CTA.

Type-specific emphasis:

- **Blog post** — 2-3 headline options; intro hook (question, statistic, bold statement, or story); SEO: primary keyword in headline and first paragraph, related keywords in subheadings.
- **Social media** — platform-appropriate format; hook in the first line; 3-5 hashtags; engagement prompt; emoji usage per brand and platform (LinkedIn: professional framing, paragraph breaks; Twitter/X: punchy, within character limit; Instagram: visual-first, story-driven).
- **Email newsletter** — 2-3 subject line options with open-rate considerations; preview text; CTA button text; unsubscribe note reminder.
- **Landing page** — headline/subheadline, hero copy, 3-4 benefit-driven value propositions, social proof placement, primary + secondary CTAs, FAQ suggestions; meta title and meta description suggestions.
- **Press release** — convention-following headline, dateline, lead (who/what/when/where/why), supporting quote placeholders, boilerplate and media contact placeholders.
- **Case study** — result-emphasizing title, customer overview, challenge, solution, results with metrics (prompt the user for data), quote placeholder, CTA.

### 4. Output and Follow-Up

Present the draft with clear formatting, then include:
- A brief note on what brand voice and tone were applied
- SEO recommendations (for web content)
- Suggested next steps (e.g., "Review with your team", "Add customer quotes", "Pair with a visual")

Ask: "Would you like me to revise any section, adjust the tone, or create a variation for a different channel?"

## Type Templates

### Blog Post
1. **Headline** — clear, benefit-driven, includes primary keyword (aim for ≤ 60 characters)
2. **Introduction** (100-150 words) — hook with a question, statistic, bold claim, or relatable scenario; state what the post covers; include primary keyword
3. **Body** (3-5 sections) — descriptive H2 subheading each; H3 for subsections; one core idea per section with evidence, examples, or data
4. **Conclusion** (75-100 words) — summarize takeaways, reinforce the message, include a CTA
5. **Meta description** — under 160 characters, includes primary keyword, compels the click

### Social Media
- **Hook** — first line grabs attention (question, bold statement, number)
- **Body** — 2-4 concise points or a short narrative
- **CTA** — what the reader should do next (comment, click, share, tag)
- **Hashtags** — 3-5 relevant, platform-dependent

### Email Newsletter
- **Subject line** — under 50 characters; curiosity or clear value
- **Preview text** — complements, does not repeat, the subject
- **Header/hero** — visual anchor + one-line value statement
- **Body** — 2-3 scannable content blocks, each with a bold intro sentence
- **Primary CTA** — one clear action, visually distinct
- **Footer** — unsubscribe link, company info, social links

### Landing Page
- **Headline** — primary benefit in under 10 words
- **Subheadline** — elaborates with supporting context
- **Hero** — headline, subheadline, primary CTA, supporting image/video
- **Value propositions** — 3-4 benefit-driven sections
- **Social proof** — testimonials, logos, stats, case study snippets
- **Objection handling** — FAQ or trust signals
- **Final CTA** — repeat the primary call to action

### Press Release
- **Headline** — factual, newsworthy, under 80 characters
- **Dateline** — city, state, date
- **Lead paragraph** — who, what, when, where, why in 2-3 sentences
- **Body paragraphs** — supporting details, quotes, context
- **Boilerplate** — standardized company description
- **Media contact** — name, email, phone

### Case Study
- **Title** — "[Customer] achieves [result] with [product]"
- **Snapshot** — customer name, industry, size, product used, key result
- **Challenge** — the problem the customer faced
- **Solution** — what was implemented and how
- **Results** — quantified outcomes with specific metrics
- **Quote** — customer testimonial
- **CTA** — learn more, get a demo, read more case studies

## Channel Best Practices

### Blog
- Write at an 8th-grade reading level for broad audiences; adjust up for technical audiences
- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences); subheadings every 200-300 words
- Bullet points and numbered lists to break up text
- At least one data point, example, or quote per section
- Active voice; front-load key information in each section

### Social Media
- **LinkedIn**: professional but human; paragraph breaks; personal stories and lessons perform well; ~1,300 characters is the sweet spot before "see more"
- **Twitter/X**: concise, punchy, strong opening words; threads for longer narratives; engage with replies
- **Instagram**: visual-first captions; storytelling hooks; line breaks; hashtags in first comment or at end
- **Facebook**: conversational; questions drive comments; shorter posts get more engagement for links

### Email
- Subject lines that create urgency, curiosity, or state clear value
- Personalize where possible (name, company, behavior)
- One primary CTA per email, visually distinct
- Scannable body: bold key phrases, short paragraphs, bullets
- Test everything: subject lines, send times, CTA copy, layout
- Mobile-first: most email is read on mobile

### Web (landing/product pages)
- Lead with benefits, not features
- "You" language — speak to the reader directly
- Minimize jargon unless the audience expects it
- Every section should answer "so what?" from the reader's perspective
- Reduce friction: fewer form fields, clear next steps, trust signals near CTAs

## SEO Fundamentals

**Keyword strategy:**
- One primary keyword and 2-3 secondary keywords per piece
- Primary keyword in: headline, first paragraph, one subheading, meta description, URL slug
- Secondary keywords naturally in body copy and subheadings
- Write for humans first — no keyword stuffing

**On-page checklist:**
- Title tag under 60 characters with primary keyword
- Meta description under 160 characters with primary keyword
- Short, descriptive URL slug with primary keyword
- One H1, matching or closely reflecting the title tag
- Descriptive H2/H3 with secondary keywords where natural
- Image alt text that is descriptive and keyword-relevant
- 2-3 internal links to related content; 1-2 external links to authoritative sources

**Integration:**
- Aim for comprehensive topic coverage (search engines reward depth)
- Answer related questions (check "People Also Ask")
- Update and refresh high-performing content regularly
- Structure for featured snippets: definition paragraphs, numbered lists, tables

## Headline and Hook Formulas

**Headlines:**
- **How to [achieve result] [without common obstacle]** — "How to Double Your Email Open Rates Without Sending More Emails"
- **[Number] [adjective] ways to [achieve result]** — "7 Proven Ways to Reduce Customer Churn"
- **Why [common belief] is wrong (and what to do instead)**
- **The [adjective] guide to [topic]**
- **[Do this], not [that]** — "Build a Community, Not Just an Audience"
- **What [impressive result] taught us about [topic]**
- **[Topic]: what [audience] needs to know in [year]**

**Hooks (opening lines):**
- **Surprising statistic**: "73% of marketers say their biggest challenge is not budget — it is focus."
- **Contrarian statement**: "The best marketing campaigns start with saying no to most channels."
- **Question**: "When was the last time a marketing email actually changed what you bought?"
- **Scenario**: "Imagine launching a campaign and knowing, before it goes live, which messages will land."
- **Bold claim**: "Most landing pages lose half their visitors in the first three seconds."
- **Story opening**: "Last quarter, our team was spending 20 hours a week on reporting. Here is what we did about it."

## Call-to-Action Best Practices

**Principles:**
- Action verbs: "Get", "Start", "Download", "Join", "Try", "See"
- Be specific about what happens next ("Start your free trial" beats "Submit")
- Create urgency only when genuine ("Join 500 teams already using this")
- Reduce risk ("No credit card required", "Cancel anytime")
- One primary CTA per page or email — too many choices reduce conversions

**Examples by context:**
- **Blog post**: "Read our complete guide to [topic]" / "Subscribe for weekly insights"
- **Landing page**: "Start free trial" / "Get a demo" / "See pricing"
- **Email**: "Read the full story" / "Claim your spot" / "Reply and tell us"
- **Social media**: "Drop a comment if you agree" / "Save this for later" / "Link in bio"
- **Case study**: "See how [product] can work for your team" / "Talk to our team"

**Placement:**
- Above the fold on landing pages (do not make users scroll to act)
- After establishing value in emails (not in the first sentence)
- At the end of blog posts (after earning the reader's trust)
- In-line when contextually relevant (a related guide mention)
- Repeat the primary CTA at the bottom of long-form pages
