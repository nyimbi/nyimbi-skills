# Operating Model and Execution

This skill owns the **how**: how the organization will work after the strategy is
decided. Design the machine — capabilities, decision rights, governance, ways of
working — then cut the initiative portfolio to what that machine can actually run, and
sequence it into delivery with owners, gates, and a first 90 days. Every design choice
traces back to a stated strategic requirement; a box on the org chart with no strategic
rationale is decoration.

Run it at the depth mode defined in
[`../strategy-diagnosis-and-framing/SKILL.md`](../strategy-diagnosis-and-framing/SKILL.md)
(Quick Structure for exploratory asks, Full Case for a real design decision, Client
Deliverable when a board or sponsor will read the output). Evidence labeling and
answer-first synthesis are that skill's contract; this skill supplies the design and
sequencing content that fills it.

## When to use

- A strategy is settled and the organization must change to deliver it: "how should we actually work?"
- Operating model design: capabilities, roles, decision rights, governance, processes, metrics, enabling systems
- Capability-gap analysis: current state vs required state, build/buy/partner calls
- Transformation or change programs: workstreams, phases, milestones, PMO setup, decision gates
- Initiative overload: annual planning, OKR planning, cost programs, product/GTM roadmaps, "which projects matter most?"
- Sequencing delivery: dependencies, readiness, first 90 days, post-merger integration
- "How do we make this happen?" — the gap between a recommendation and a delivery plan

**Do not use for** — `strategic-choices-and-economics` when the question is which
option to pick and what it is worth (option comparison, business cases, portfolio
allocation); `risk-performance-and-value-governance` when the question is how you will
know it is working and what could break it — KPI systems, value ledgers, risk
registers, war games (this skill designs the governance *forum*, that skill fills its
agenda); `executive-communication-and-alignment` when the recommendation exists and the
job is winning the decision, the memo, or the pre-wire. The fact base and root-cause
diagnosis that should precede any redesign belong to
`strategy-diagnosis-and-framing`.

## Workflow

1. **Clarify the mandate before designing anything.** Establish the **decision**
   (what is being designed and who approves it), the **strategic requirement** the
   model must serve, the **success metric**, **constraints** (time, budget,
   capability, politics, regulatory), and **scope** (functions, entities, geographies
   in and out). Context check: decision-maker, success metric, and scope boundaries
   identified or explicitly assumed. If any are missing, state **provisional
   assumptions [A]** and proceed — never return questions without a design. Output: a
   one-line mandate plus assumption list.

2. **Build the capability model from the strategy down.** Translate the requirement
   into the capabilities it demands, then assess each: current state, required state,
   gap, and whether to build, buy, partner, or skip. Output: a capability gap table.
   Use 7S when the gap looks like an organizational root cause rather than a process
   gap; pick the framework from
   [`../strategy-diagnosis-and-framing/references/frameworks-index.md`](../strategy-diagnosis-and-framing/references/frameworks-index.md)
   and check its "when NOT to use" note first.

3. **Design decision rights and governance around the gaps.** For each load-bearing
   decision — pricing, hiring, roadmap, capital, escalations — name one **owner**,
   contributors, what they must be consulted on, and the escalation path. Then design
   the forums: cadence, who attends, what gets decided, what inputs they need.
   RAPID fits most decision-rights tables. Governance *structure* lives here; the KPIs
   and benefits that fill each forum's agenda come from
   [`../risk-performance-and-value-governance/SKILL.md`](../risk-performance-and-value-governance/SKILL.md)
   — do not design metrics twice. Output: decision-rights table plus forum design.

4. **Design the change, not just the boxes.** Organizations do not adopt operating
   models; people change behavior. Segment impacted groups, pick the change approach
   (ADKAR for adoption stalls, Kotter for urgency-and-coalition programs, Lewin for
   bounded process change, Influence Model for culture levers), and name the
   transition risks, capability builds, and quick wins that protect throughput during
   the move. Practitioner detail, including good-vs-bad outputs and breakdown
   conditions:
   [`references/frameworks-operations.md`](references/frameworks-operations.md).

5. **Prioritize the initiative portfolio to what the design can run.** Inventory every
   initiative, score against **impact, feasibility, urgency, strategic fit**, and
   dependencies, then force the cut: vital few, defer list, and a kill list with
   rationale and a **revisit trigger** (the event that would justify reopening it).
   Check the shortlist against real capacity — headcount, capital, management
   bandwidth — and drop or defer what does not fit. Output: scored assessment and kill
   list. **Deduplication:** scoring, fit, and the kill decision happen only here;
   phase ordering and timing do not — step 6 owns sequencing.

6. **Sequence the survivors into a roadmap with gates.** Order phases by **dependency,
   value release, and readiness**; group into workstreams with objectives, owners, and
   key dependencies; set milestones and decision gates (continue / re-scope / stop);
   write the first 90 days as concrete actions with named owners. Output: phased
   roadmap, 90-day plan, and governance cadence. **Deduplication:** the prioritizer
   supplies the ordered input set; the roadmap owns phases, gates, timing, and the
   governance calendar — one concept, one home.

7. **Challenge the design before you ship it (devil's advocate, mandatory).** Argue
   against your own answer: strongest reason this model fails in year one; the
   assumption whose failure reverses it; what a skeptical operator or hostile rival
   would say. Pre-mortem and inversion are the fastest tests
   ([`../strategic-choices-and-economics/references/frameworks-decision.md`](../strategic-choices-and-economics/references/frameworks-decision.md)).
   Risks that would change the approval decision get scored into a register —
   that is `risk-performance-and-value-governance`'s artifact; link to it, do not
   rebuild it here.

8. **Synthesize answer-first, then verify.** Lead with the bottom line (the design and
   the sequence in one sentence), then at most three load-bearing arguments with
   evidence labeled `[F]/[I]/[A]/[E]`, then risks, kill conditions, next actions with
   owners and dates, and open questions — the full contract lives in
   [`../strategy-diagnosis-and-framing/SKILL.md`](../strategy-diagnosis-and-framing/SKILL.md).
   Run the quality bar below before delivering; deliver the templates in Output
   format for whatever the ask produced (design, prioritization, roadmap, or all
   three as one pack).

## Quality bar

Verify before delivering:

- [ ] Every design element traces to a stated strategic requirement; nothing exists only because org charts look like that
- [ ] Capability table states current, required, and gap explicitly — no implied gaps
- [ ] Every load-bearing decision has one named owner, contributors, and an escalation path; no shared ownership
- [ ] Governance specifies forum, cadence, decisions made, and required inputs — not just "steerco monthly"
- [ ] Prioritization is forced: not everything is high priority, and a kill list exists with rationale and revisit trigger
- [ ] Shortlist fits stated capacity (people, capital, management attention) or the overflow is explicitly deferred
- [ ] Sequencing shows dependencies and readiness; first 90 days, milestones, and decision gates are explicit
- [ ] Every initiative, phase, and workstream has an owner and a success metric
- [ ] Transition risks and capability-build needs are named with mitigation, not footnoted
- [ ] Devil's advocate is on the page: strongest counter-argument and the assumption that would reverse the design
- [ ] Evidence labeled `[F]/[I]/[A]/[E]`; every number carries unit, period, and source

## Output format

Deliver the template that matches the ask. A full engagement delivers all three as
one pack: design first, prioritization second, roadmap third. Prefix the pack with the
answer-first synthesis from step 8.

```markdown
# Operating Model Design

## Strategic Requirement
[What the model must enable, and the success metric.]

## Capability Model
| Capability | Current State | Required State | Gap | Build / Buy / Partner |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Target Model
| Element | Recommendation | Rationale (strategy link) |
|---|---|---|

## Decision Rights
| Decision | Owner | Contributors | Governance Forum | Escalation |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Transition Plan
[Phases, change approach, risks, capability builds, first moves.]
```

```markdown
# Initiative Prioritization

## Criteria
[Decision criteria and weights; capacity constraint stated.]

## Initiative Assessment
| Initiative | Impact | Feasibility | Fit | Urgency | Dependencies | Priority | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Priority Roadmap
| Phase | Initiative | Owner | Milestone |
|---|---|---|---|

## Kill List
| Initiative | Reason | Revisit Trigger |
|---|---|---|
```

```markdown
# Transformation Roadmap

## Target Outcome
[What must change, by when, and how success is measured.]

## Workstreams
| Workstream | Objective | Owner | Key Dependency |
|---|---|---|---|

## Roadmap
| Phase | Timing | Major Actions | Milestone | Decision Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## First 90 Days
[Immediate actions, owners, and the first gate.]

## Governance
[Cadence, escalation, metrics on the agenda, and owners.]
```
