# Static Composition — Philosophy to PNG/PDF

Technique file for the static-poster branch of the [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) workflow.
canvas-design supplies **static composition**: an aesthetic position, then one
high-craft visual artifact delivered as `.png` or `.pdf`. Files: `.md` (philosophy),
then `.png`/`.pdf` (artifact).

## Step A — Write the design philosophy first

A visual philosophy, not a layout or a template. Output as a `.md` file.

1. **Name the movement** in 1–2 words — "Brutalist Joy", "Chromatic Silence",
   "Metabolist Dreams".
2. **Articulate it in 4–6 substantial paragraphs**, covering how the position shows up
   in: space and form · colour and material · scale and rhythm · composition and
   balance · visual hierarchy. Each aspect once — no repeating the same colour-theory
   point twice. Leave interpretive room: state the direction firmly enough to constrain
   choices, loosely enough that the next pass can make its own high-level decisions.
3. **Keep it generic.** It should be usable for any brief that shares the position —
   it describes the aesthetic worldview, not the artwork's subject.

Condensed starting positions: *Concrete Poetry* (monumental form, bold geometry, text
as rare powerful gesture) · *Chromatic Language* (colour as the information system) ·
*Analog Meditation* (paper grain, ink bleed, negative space) · *Organic Systems*
(rounded forms, modular growth) · *Geometric Silence* (grid precision, dramatic
restraint).

## Step B — Deduce the embedded reference

Before touching the canvas, identify the subtle conceptual thread in the original
request. It is a quiet DNA woven into form, colour, and composition — felt by someone
who knows the subject, invisible to everyone else, who just sees a well-made piece. Like
a jazz musician quoting a song: only those who know will catch it, and everyone still
enjoys the music. Never announce it literally.

## Step C — Compose the canvas

- **One page** as a default (more only if asked). Highly visual, design-forward; text
  is 10% at most and never a paragraph.
- Use repeating patterns, perfect shapes, and systematic marks — dense accumulation
  that rewards sustained viewing. Sparse clinical typography and reference markers make
  the piece read like a plate from an imaginary discipline.
- Anchor with one or two short phrases, positioned with intent, inside a limited
  palette that feels chosen rather than sampled.
- **Typography is part of the art.** Choose from `../canvas-fonts/` (each family ships
  with its licence file) — bring the type onto the canvas rather than typesetting it
  digitally. Text scale follows the subject: a punk venue poster may take large
  aggressive type where a ceramics identity whispers.
- **Containment is non-negotiable:** nothing overlaps, nothing clips, everything sits
  inside the margins with clear separation. Verify this explicitly before delivery.
- Original work only — never reproduce a living artist's piece.

## Step D — Refine, do not decorate

The second pass is a refinement pass. Do not add graphics, filters, or new fonts.
Ask instead: *how does what is already here become more of a piece of art?* Tighten
alignment, kerning, spacing, and colour cohesion until the composition reads as one
object. Multi-page requests extend the philosophy with distinctly different pages that
still read as one book — first page is a single plate in a coffee-table volume.

## Deliverables

`movement-name.md` (the philosophy) alongside `artifact.png` or `artifact.pdf`. If the
raster needs more resolution for its destination, run the export step in
[image-production.md](image-production.md).
