# Executive Communication and Alignment

This skill owns **the win**: the decision on the table and the people who must grant
it. The analysis is done elsewhere — `strategy-diagnosis-and-framing` builds the fact
base, `strategic-choices-and-economics` compares and values the options,
`operating-model-and-execution` defines the change. Here the job is to make the
recommendation land, in writing, with the right people already on side.

Three artifacts, three jobs, one recommendation (each defined once):

- **Decision memo** — the *document* that lets one decision-maker act without a
  meeting: recommendation, options, evidence, risks, decision requested.
- **Narrative** — the *storyline* for a deck or a room: headlines, pyramid logic,
  SCQA when context is needed, hostile questions prepped.
- **Stakeholder alignment** — the *campaign* that makes both work: who must say yes,
  what each needs to believe, what happens before the formal meeting.

Run at the depth mode defined in
[`../strategy-diagnosis-and-framing/SKILL.md`](../strategy-diagnosis-and-framing/SKILL.md);
memo and narrative deliverables run at Client Deliverable depth.

## When to use

- A written recommendation for a leader, board, or investment committee: "write this up for leadership", "draft a decision memo", "board paper", "IC memo"
- The analysis is done and the story must land: "build the storyline", "make this land", presentation structure, executive narrative
- Approval must be won across people: "how do we get this approved?", pre-wiring, ExCo alignment, board approval, investor narrative
- Preparing for hostile questioning: skeptic prep, objection handling, "what will they push back on?"
- Translating the same progress for different audiences: exec brief vs steering detail
- Sequencing engagement before a formal meeting — who is spoken to, when, by whom

**Do not use for** — `strategy-diagnosis-and-framing` when the fact base, root cause,
or recommendation itself is still missing (this skill shapes an existing
recommendation; it does not create one); `strategic-choices-and-economics` when the
options must still be compared or valued; `market-and-competitive-intelligence` when
the competitive or market evidence must still be gathered;
`presentation-authoring` when the ask is the finished deck artifact — layout, theme,
slides — rather than the argument on them; `writing-and-internal-communications` for
routine updates, announcements, and company-wide copy; `research-and-insight-analysis`
for meeting or lead analytics. Organizational change itself is
`operating-model-and-execution`.

## Workflow

1. **Clarify the decision, the audience, and the path it takes.** Establish: what
   **decision** must be made, by **whom**, by **when**, what **action** approval
   triggers, and the formal path (who decides, who only influences). Context check:
   decision-maker, success metric, and scope identified or assumed; if missing, state
   **provisional assumptions [A]** and draft anyway — never stall a memo on missing
   detail. Output: one-line brief (decision, audience, deadline, path).

2. **Map the room before writing a word.** For each stakeholder: **role** in the
   decision, **influence** (real influence, not org-chart rank), **current stance**
   (supporter / neutral / blocker), **decision criteria** — what they will judge it
   by — and **what they need to believe**. List resistance points with the response
   each deserves. Output: stakeholder map; this is the input to the pre-wire plan in
   step 6.

3. **Extract the recommendation and its evidence.** Bottom line in one sentence,
   then at most **three load-bearing arguments**, each carrying evidence labeled
   `[F]/[I]/[A]/[E]` with unit, period, and source
   ([`../strategy-diagnosis-and-framing/references/evidence-standards.md`](../strategy-diagnosis-and-framing/references/evidence-standards.md)).
   Summarize the **options considered** and why each lost — with the *same* rigor
   applied to the runner-up; an alternative that never faced the same test is a
   default, not a loser. Option analysis itself is
   `strategic-choices-and-economics`' output; cite it, don't redo it. Output:
   recommendation block plus options table.

4. **Choose the story pattern and build the headline sequence.** Pick one: **Pyramid**
   (answer, then arguments) for decisions; **SCQA** when the audience needs context
   and urgency first; **options** when the decision is comparative;
   **transformation** or **roadmap** when the ask is to endorse a change. Write every
   headline so it stands alone — a reader who sees only the headlines gets the whole
   argument. The recommendation must be visible in the first minute or the first
   page. Output: ordered headline list with the purpose of each.

5. **Draft the deliverable.** Reach for the **decision memo** when one person must act
   on paper (template below); reach for the **narrative outline** when the medium is a
   deck or a room (template below). Same recommendation and evidence either way —
   written once, shaped twice; the memo compresses, the narrative sequences. Keep the
   memo to what changes the decision; delete setup language the reader already knows.

6. **Build the pre-wire plan.** Sequence the engagement *before* the formal meeting:
   who is spoken to first (usually the blockers and the quiet influencers), by whom,
   with what message, what objection is expected, the response, and the desired
   outcome by the time the meeting starts. Output: pre-wire table with owner, timing,
   and target stance. Alignment that starts in the meeting has already failed.

7. **Prepare the hostile questions, then challenge your own draft (mandatory).**
   List the questions a skeptical executive will actually ask — the uncomfortable
   ones — and answer each with evidence, not reassurance. Then argue against your own
   recommendation: strongest reason it is wrong, the assumption that reverses it, what
   a smart opponent says. Put the counter-argument on the page and either defend
   against it or revise. Pre-mortem and inversion speed this up:
   [`../strategic-choices-and-economics/references/frameworks-decision.md`](../strategic-choices-and-economics/references/frameworks-decision.md).

8. **Verify against the quality bar, then deliver.** Run the checks below; deliver the
   template(s) from Output format. Risks cited in the memo must be the register's top
   rows, not fresh prose — the register lives in
   [`../risk-performance-and-value-governance/SKILL.md`](../risk-performance-and-value-governance/SKILL.md).

## Quality bar

Verify before delivering:

- [ ] Recommendation is on the first line or first page; nothing precedes the answer
- [ ] The decision requested is explicit: what to approve or choose, and by when
- [ ] At most three load-bearing arguments, each labeled `[F]/[I]/[A]/[E]` with sourced numbers
- [ ] Options table gives the runner-up the same rigor as the winner — or the memo says so
- [ ] At least two risks with mitigations and two kill conditions carried from the analysis
- [ ] Every stakeholder has stance, influence, decision criteria, and what they need to believe
- [ ] Pre-wire actions are sequenced before the formal meeting, each with owner and timing
- [ ] Hostile questions are answered with evidence; the counter-argument is stated on the page
- [ ] Headlines stand alone and tell the story in order; no setup language that does not move the decision
- [ ] Memo fits on the page an executive will actually read — concise, answer-first, ends with the ask

## Output format

Deliver the memo, the narrative, or both — plus the pre-wire plan whenever approval
must be won from more than one person.

```markdown
# Decision Memo

## Recommendation
[One paragraph — answer first, what and why.]

## Decision Required
[What the reader must approve or choose, and by when.]

## Context
[Why this matters now — situation and complication, two sentences.]

## Options Considered
| Option | Upside | Trade-Off | Why It Lost / Why It Wins |
|---|---|---|---|

## Evidence
[Three load-bearing arguments max, each labeled [F]/[I]/[A]/[E] with source.]

## Risks and Mitigations
[Material risks from the register — likelihood, impact, mitigation, owner.]

## Next Steps
[Immediate actions after approval — owner, date, success metric.]
```

```markdown
# Executive Narrative

## Audience and Decision
[Who must decide, what they must do, by when.]

## Core Message
[One sentence — the recommendation.]

## Storyline
| Page or Section | Headline (stands alone) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|

## Pyramid Logic
[Recommendation → up to three supporting arguments → evidence with labels.]

## Hostile Questions
| Question | Answer (evidence, not reassurance) |
|---|---|

## Counter-Argument
[Strongest reason this is wrong, and why the recommendation still holds — or what changed.]
```

```markdown
# Stakeholder Alignment Plan

## Decision Path
[Who decides, who influences, and when.]

## Stakeholder Map
| Stakeholder | Role | Influence | Current Stance | Decision Criteria | What They Need To Believe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Resistance Points
| Concern | Who Holds It | Response |
|---|---|---|

## Pre-Wire Plan
| Action | Owner | Timing | Desired Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
```
