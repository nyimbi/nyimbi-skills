What is the best AI image model for readable text in images?

FLORA Team

In our FLORA test, GPT Image 2.5 Flare, Ideogram 4.5 and Nano Banana 2.1 all spelled a five-line cafe menu correctly. GPT Image 2.5 Flare kept every line exactly as written for $0.055, so it is the one to start with. FLORA runs all three on one canvas, so you can check the spelling side by side before you pick.

Key takeaways

  • All three models spelled all five menu lines correctly, prices included, on the first try.
  • GPT Image 2.5 Flare kept the line breaks as written; Nano Banana 2.1 wrapped two lines onto a second row.
  • Ideogram 4.5 cost $0.12 and returned a 1728 x 2304 image; the other two cost $0.052 to $0.055 at about 900 x 1200.
  • The whole three-model test cost $0.227 and each image took 28 to 35 seconds.
  • Quote the exact words in the prompt and put each line on its own line.

    Best for: Fast, low-cost text images and edits at volume

What happened when we tested text rendering in FLORA?

On October 16, 2026, we sent one prompt to three models in a single flora_create_generations call, all at 3:4: "Photo of a chalkboard menu hanging on a white tiled wall in a small bakery cafe, soft morning daylight. The board reads, in neat hand-lettered chalk, exactly:" followed by five lines: MORNING MENU / Oat flat white 4.50 / Cardamom bun 3.75 / Rye toast, cultured butter 6.00 / Open 7am to 2pm.

Settings: t2i-gpt-image-2-5-flare at quality high and 1K; t2i-ideogram-4-5-t2i at quality high with magic prompt off; t2i-elote-gateway (Nano Banana 2.1) at 1K. We read all three with one flora_list_generations call. Costs: $0.055, $0.12 and $0.052, for $0.227 in total. Times: 35.3, 32.2 and 28.3 seconds.

Every word and price came back right in all three. The differences were layout. GPT Image 2.5 Flare matched the line breaks exactly and aligned the prices in a column. Ideogram 4.5 did the same, with a larger title. Nano Banana 2.1 split "MORNING MENU" and "Rye toast, cultured butter" across two rows each.

What went wrong: nothing in the spelling, but the Nano Banana 2.1 wrap would push a price onto the wrong row on a busier menu. The fix is to state the layout too, such as "each item and its price on one line", or to keep the GPT Image 2.5 result. On longer text blocks, check every word anyway: one wrong letter is enough to sink a print run.

How do I write a prompt that gets the text right?

Give the model the exact words and where they go.

  • Say "reads exactly:" and then give the text.
  • Put each line of text on its own line in the prompt.
  • State the layout: alignment, which line is biggest, what sits on one line.
  • Keep it short. Five lines worked cleanly; a paragraph is harder for any model.
  • Name the lettering style, such as hand-lettered chalk or a simple serif.

What if one word comes back wrong?

Edit, don't regenerate. Ask for an image-to-image edit with the result as the reference and a note such as "change Cardamon to Cardamom, keep everything else". Each GPT Image 2.5 edit costs about $0.064, and the rest of the image stays put.

Should text go in the image or be added later?

For mockups, concepts and social posts, in-image text is fast and now reliable on short copy. For final packaging or legal copy, set the type in a design tool on top of a clean image, which you can ask FLORA for with "no text" in the prompt.

Which AI image model should you pick for readable text?

On short text, the top models are all accurate now; layout, size and cost separate them. Running them side by side in FLORA is the quickest way to see which one fits the job.

  • Not sure which model fits, or need several looks: FLORA, with all of them on one canvas.
  • Exact line breaks and layout: GPT Image 2.5 Flare.
  • Type-led posters and packaging at a larger native size: Ideogram 4.5.
  • Fast, cheap text images at volume: Nano Banana 2.1.
  • Text inside a logo or vector illustration: Recraft V4.
