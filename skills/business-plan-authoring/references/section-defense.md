# Section Defense — Business Plan Template

The full plan structure. For each section: **must establish** (what the
section owes the reader), **the standard hostile attack** (what a skeptical
funder probes), and **pre-empt** (how the section disarms it before being
asked). Write the counter-argument *into* the plan — a plan that states its own
strongest objection and survives it is worth more than one that never meets
one.

Order is fixed except the executive summary, which is written last.

---

## 1. Executive summary (write last)

**Must establish:** what the company does, for whom, the market size, why now,
traction to date, the ask (amount, instrument), what the money buys (named
milestone), and why this team. Answer-first, one to two pages.

**Hostile attack:** "Give me the version you'd send to someone who will not
read the rest." If the summary needs the body to be believed, it fails.

**Pre-empt:** every number in the summary must be traceable to a claim in the
evidence ledger. The ask names the milestone the money buys — "Series A of
$[X] to reach [recurring revenue] and [cohort] by [date]" — so the funder
evaluates the plan against a stated target, not an implied one.

## 2. Problem

**Must establish:** the pain, who has it, how often, what it costs them today,
and that they currently solve it badly (workaround, incumbent, or nothing) —
with evidence the reader can verify.

**Hostile attack:** "Is this a real problem or a solution looking for a
problem? Would they pay, or just nod sympathetically?"

**Pre-empt:** lead with observed behavior, not stated need — switching costs
incurred, budget already spent, workarounds maintained. One dated quote per
persona is not evidence; the pattern behind ten is.

## 3. Solution and product

**Must establish:** what it is, what the customer does with it, what is shipped
today versus planned, and the mechanism of value (why it removes or reduces the
pain, not just "better").

**Hostile attack:** "What happens if the incumbent ships this feature? What is
the durable part of the product?"

**Pre-empt:** separate the durable mechanism (workflow capture, data, network
effect, switching cost, regulation) from the feature list. Name what would have
to be true for the incumbent to make this uncompetitive, and why that is
costly for them (cannibalization, channel conflict, architectural change).

## 4. Market

**Must establish:** TAM/SAM/SOM sized **two ways** (top-down and bottom-up)
with the gap explained; growth over three years with sources; where the profit
sits; which segment is attacked first and why.

**Hostile attack:** "Your TAM is the whole market and your SOM is a leap. Show
me the math on the number you actually believe."

**Pre-empt:** present the bottom-up number as the plan of record and the
top-down as context. If the two differ by 2x+, say why. The SOM must be
reachable: name the segment, the channel, and the number of units in it —
"[N] firms in [segment] reachable through [channel] at [price] = [SOM]".

## 5. Business model and unit economics

**Must establish:** how money is made (pricing model, contract length,
expansion), and the derived unit economics: CAC (by channel), LTV (with its
assumptions), payback period, and gross margin **with its cost drivers named**
(infra, delivery, payments, data).

**Hostile attack:** "Show me your CAC payback. Is gross margin a claim or a
cost model? Are you buying revenue?"

**Pre-empt:** derive, don't assert. "CAC of $[x] = (sales comp + tooling +
events) ÷ [closed-won in period]" beats "CAC is about $x". State which margin
is current and which is the 24-month target, and the cost lever that closes the
gap. If payback exceeds 12 months, the plan must say why the investor should
fund the gap — not hide the number.

## 6. Competition

**Must establish:** the honest set — incumbent, adjacent, manual/Excel, and
do-nothing — each with their strength and their ceiling; and a model of how
the strongest one responds to a successful version of this company.

**Hostile attack:** "Your competitive slide has no entry for the incumbent. And
why won't they just crush you in six months?"

**Pre-empt:** a competitive matrix that lists the incumbent's advantages
first — a matrix that hides them is read as either naivety or dishonesty.
Then the modeled response: if [incumbent] matches the feature, they
[price cut / bundle / re-architect], which costs them [X] and takes [time];
our position survives on [mechanism].

## 7. Go-to-market

**Must establish:** the channel(s), the motion (sales-led / product-led /
partner), the evidence it works for this segment, the pipeline or early
traction, and the cost structure of the channel.

**Hostile attack:** "That CAC is a lab number from three friends. What happens
when it scales? Who else in the channel has this?"

**Pre-empt:** state the sample size of the CAC claim explicitly. Separate
founder-driven wins from channel-driven wins — hostile readers discount
founder sales heavily and reward reproducible pipeline. If a channel partner is
named, the plan states what is signed versus hoped.

## 8. Team

**Must establish:** why this team specifically wins in this market — relevant
domain evidence (built, sold, operated in this space before; data, customers,
regulatory access), and the gaps filled by named advisors or a hiring plan.

**Hostile attack:** "Why this team and not [famous founder]? What have you
actually done in this market?"

**Pre-empt:** concrete evidence over pedigree: "ran [function] at [company]
where [metric]", "has [N] customers in this segment from prior work". Name the
gap honestly ("we need a [function] hire in [quarter]; [named candidate /
source] is the plan") — a team section without one acknowledged gap reads as
arrogance.

## 9. Traction

**Must establish:** what is real — revenue, customers, waitlist, pilots — with
cohort context: retention/expansion if it exists, churn if it exists, and the
shape of the recent trend, not just the headline.

**Hostile attack:** "That's survivorship — one design partner who owes you.
Show me the cohort. Show me the churn."

**Pre-empt:** present the cohort table even when it is small; state which
revenue is committed versus pilot. If churn exists, the plan explains it
before being asked. Traction with visible churn and a working retention story
beats smooth traction with no cohort view.

## 10. Financials and the ask

**Must establish:** the 24–36 month driver-based model (see
`projections.md`), the ask (amount, instrument, valuation mechanics where
appropriate), the use of funds as a burn bridge to the named milestone, and
what happens at the milestone (next raise, break-even, decision point).

**Hostile attack:** "This is a hockey stick. Change CAC by 20% — what breaks?
And why is this the right amount of money?"

**Pre-empt:** show the scenario sensitivity (change the two most load-bearing
drivers, show the output) — see `hostile-review.md`. The use-of-funds table
matches the hiring plan line by line. State the milestone the round buys and
the valuation logic or decision point at the end of the bridge.

## 11. Risks and mitigations

**Must establish:** the plan's **three weakest points**, named explicitly, each
with mitigation and kill condition — plus the top external risks (regulatory,
concentration, key person, technology) with the plan's response.

**Hostile attack:** "This risk section is a list of things that can't really
happen. What is your actual worst case?"

**Pre-empt:** the worst case, stated. If the single most likely failure is a
key-person dependency or a concentration in one customer, it is here with the
mitigation and the kill condition ("if [customer] churns, we trigger [plan]").
A risk section a hostile reader can beat is a liability; one they cannot is an
asset.

## 12. Use of funds (table)

**Must establish:** category-level allocation (product, go-to-market, ops,
buffer) mapped to quarters, tied to the hiring and milestone plan, with the
buffer explicit.

**Hostile attack:** "Why is buffer 20%? Show me the burn bridge."

**Pre-empt:** the burn bridge: starting cash + round − burn = months to
milestone, with the milestone and the next decision point named. The buffer is
stated with the contingency it covers.

---

## Writing rules that apply to every section

- **Answer-first.** Each section opens with its claim in one sentence; the
  evidence follows. (Pyramid Principle — see
  `../../executive-communication-and-alignment/SKILL.md`.)
- **One number, one tag.** Every quantitative statement carries a claim
  number or tag from the evidence ledger.
- **Counter-argument inline.** Each section ends with "Strongest
  counter-argument and our answer" — two or three sentences, not a dodge.
- **No hedging as a strategy.** "We believe", "potentially", "world-class"
  without evidence are deleted, not softened.
